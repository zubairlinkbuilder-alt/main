    What this number is — and is not

    It is an apparent age: the age a model trained on labelled face photos reads your face as. It is not your biological age, it is not a health measurement, and it says nothing about how long you will live. Any tool that tells you otherwise from a single selfie is overselling itself.

    How the estimate works

    1. Face detection. The model locates every face in the photo and keeps the largest one.
    2. Landmark mapping. 68 points are placed across the brows, eyes, nose, mouth and jawline to align the face.
    3. Age prediction. A neural network trained on labelled-age photographs returns a single number, which is shown here as a range.

    All three steps run on your own device. The model file is downloaded to your browser once and the photo is never sent anywhere.

    How accurate is an AI age detector?

    Accurate enough to be entertaining, not accurate enough to rely on. Published vendor benchmarks are honest about where the error sits: one age-assurance provider reports an overall mean error of about 1.6 years, but the same benchmark shows 3.3 years at ages 26–40 and 5.0 years at 40 and over. Error grows with age, because adult faces change far more slowly than children's.

    That is why the result here is shown as a band rather than a single confident number. Expect it to move by several years between two photos of the same person taken on the same day.

    What pushes the estimate up or down

    • Lighting. Harsh overhead light deepens shadows under the eyes and mouth and reads as older. Soft, even front light reads younger.
    • Camera angle. A phone held low and close widens the jaw and adds apparent weight to the lower face.
    • Resolution. Low-resolution photos smear the fine skin detail the model reads as youth.
    • Facial hair. Beards consistently push estimates upward.
    • Expression. A neutral face reads older than a smile.
    • Make-up and glasses. Both shift the result, in either direction.

    How to get the most accurate result

    1. Face the camera straight on, with your whole face in frame.
    2. Use soft, even light from in front of you — never from directly overhead or behind.
    3. Hold the camera at eye level, at arm's length.
    4. Take off sunglasses and pull hair back from your forehead and jaw.
    5. Keep a neutral expression, and use the sharpest photo you have.

    Questions people ask

    How accurate is an AI age detector?

    Models of this type are typically a few years out either way, and the error grows with age — estimates for children are tighter than for adults over 40, where several vendors report an average error of five years or more. Treat the number as a range, not a reading.

    Is this my biological age?

    No. Biological age is estimated from clinical and molecular markers, and the published research on predicting health outcomes from face photos comes from hospital datasets with consent procedures attached. This tool estimates how old your face looks. The two are not the same thing and should not be read as one.

    Why did it say I look older than I am?

    Usually the photo, not the face. Harsh overhead light deepens shadows around the eyes and mouth; a low camera angle adds weight to the jaw; low resolution smears the fine skin detail the model reads as youth. Facial hair pushes estimates up, and a neutral expression reads older than a smile. Try a second photo in soft, even, front-on light.

    Does my photo get uploaded?

    No. The model file downloads to your browser and the photo is processed on your device. Nothing about your image is transmitted, so there is no copy of it anywhere but your own phone or computer.

    Can I use it to verify someone's age?

    No, and you should not. Age verification and age assurance are a regulated field with its own accuracy and auditing requirements. This is an entertainment tool for your own photo.
