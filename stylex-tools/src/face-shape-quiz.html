          What your face structure actually means

          Every face shape is a judgement about four lines and one ratio. Learn them once and you can read any face, including your own, without a tape measure.

          • Forehead width. Measured across the widest point, roughly a finger above the eyebrows.
          • Cheekbone width. Across the face at the outer corners of your eyes, where the bone sits highest.
          • Jaw width. From one gonial angle to the other, the corners below your ears where the jaw turns.
          • Face length. Hairline to the base of the chin.
          • The chin. Pointed, rounded or square, which decides several shapes on its own.

          Two questions settle most faces. Which of the three widths is largest? And how does the length compare to the width? A face longer than it is wide with even widths is oblong. One as wide as it is long with a hard jaw is square. Forehead widest with a pointed chin is heart. Jaw widest is triangle.

          You may also see names that are not separate shapes at all. Rectangle is oblong. Long face is oblong. Pear is triangle. Inverted triangle is heart. Apple face shape is a make-up term for a round face with full cheeks, not a bone-structure category. If a quiz offers it, read it as round.

          How to determine your face shape for a haircut

          A barber does not need the label. They need to know which proportion you want to change. That is the useful translation:

          1. Find your widest point. Forehead, cheekbones or jaw. This decides whether a cut should add or remove width, and where.
          2. Compare length to width. A long face wants width at the sides. A wide face wants height on top.
          3. Read the chin. A pointed chin takes weight near the jaw; a heavy jaw takes volume above the eyeline.
          4. Say it in the chair. "My face is long and narrow, so keep some width at the temples" works better than "I have an oblong face".

          Does face shape differ for men and women?

          The bone structure categories are identical: the same seven shapes, judged the same way. What changes is the cut that answers them, because the silhouettes differ. A round face wants height and a tight perimeter either way, but for men that usually means a fade with volume on top, and for women it usually means long layers starting below the chin and a deep side part. That is why the result above has both sets.

          One real difference: facial hair. A beard is the fastest way to change the apparent shape of the lower face, which makes it a genuine styling tool for a round, heart or diamond face, and a trap for a triangle or square one.

          How accurate is a face shape quiz?

          Honestly: good enough to point you at the right haircut, not precise enough to settle an argument. A quiz is reading your judgement of your own face, and people routinely misjudge their own forehead width and jaw angle. It also cannot see you.

          Where a quiz does better than a tape measure is the borderline. Most faces are not a clean example of one shape. They sit between two, and the cuts that appear on both lists are the safe ones. That is why this quiz reports a close second rather than forcing a single label.

          If you want a number rather than a judgement, measure the four widths and compare the ratios. The face shape calculator does that arithmetic, and takes a photo if you would rather not hold a tape to your head.

          Questions people ask

          What face type am I if I'm between two shapes?

          Both, in practice. The seven shapes are a stylist's shorthand, not biological categories, and most faces borrow from two. When this quiz finds a close call it names both, and the cuts that appear on both lists are the ones that will work on you.

          How do I know my face shape without measuring?

          Answer two questions in a mirror with your hair pulled back. Which is widest: forehead, cheekbones or jaw? And is your face longer than it is wide, or about the same? Those two answers narrow seven shapes to two, and your chin shape usually settles it. That is exactly what this quiz does, with five more questions to catch the edge cases.

          Can my face shape change?

          The bone structure does not, but what you see does. Weight changes alter cheek fullness and jaw definition, and ageing softens the jawline and can make a square face read oval. Facial hair changes the lower third outright. Re-checking every year or so is reasonable.

          Is a face shape quiz as accurate as a photo detector?

          Different kinds of error. A quiz depends on how well you judge your own face; a photo tool depends on the photo, because camera angle, lens distance and lighting all shift the result. Measurements are the most reliable of the three. If the quiz and a detector disagree, you are probably between two shapes.

          What is the apple face shape?

          A make-up term, not a bone-structure category. It describes a round face with full cheeks and a soft jaw. If a chart offers it, treat it as round and use the round recommendations.

          Which face shape is best for haircuts?

          Oval, only because it is the most forgiving, since its proportions fight the fewest cuts. That is not the same as better looking, and every other shape has cuts that suit it better than they suit an oval face. The goal is never to look oval; it is to pick a cut that works with the proportions you have.
