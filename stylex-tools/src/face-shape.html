          Questions people ask

          How accurate is a face shape detector?

          Shape is a ratio judgement, not a fact, and the tool is measuring a 2D photo of a 3D head. A straight-on photo in even light with hair off the face gives a reliable read. A turned head, a low camera angle, a wide-angle phone lens held close, or hair across the forehead will all shift the numbers. The tool flags those conditions when it detects them, and shows the ratios so you can judge the call yourself.

          What are the seven face shapes?

          Oval, round, square, oblong (also called rectangle), heart, diamond and triangle (also called pear). They are defined by which part of the face is widest (forehead, cheekbones or jaw), how the length compares to the width, and whether the chin is pointed or rounded.

          Why does it say I'm between two shapes?

          Because most people are. The seven categories are a stylist's shorthand, not biological groups. If your top two bars are close, read both sets of recommendations, because the cuts that appear on both lists are your safest options.

          Does my photo get uploaded?

          No. The detection model is downloaded to your browser and the photo is measured on your own device. Nothing about your image is sent anywhere, so there is nothing to store or delete.

          How should I take the photo?

          Face the camera straight on with a neutral expression. Pull hair back so the hairline, ears and jaw are visible. Use even front light, because overhead light casts shadows that distort the jaw. Hold the phone at eye level and at arm's length; held close and low, a phone lens widens the jaw and shortens the face.
